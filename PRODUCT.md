# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary user is an overseas board-repair technician working at a bench with a faulty phone mainboard, point maps, a multimeter, and limited time. The interface must help the technician identify the board, locate a suspected module or test point, carry out the next check, and record the result without first understanding the project structure or reading long technical pages.

## Product Purpose

Turn verified in-house board materials into a visual guided-repair workflow. A technician selects a model and board version, opens the board atlas, chooses a known fault or starts an initial board check, sees the likely areas on the board, and follows source-based measurement guidance. Success means the board image becomes the main working surface and text supports the action instead of replacing it.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, dependable. The product should feel like a professional bench instrument: dense enough for repeated technical work, quiet enough to keep attention on the board, and explicit about the current state and source material.

## Anti-references

- Text-heavy knowledge portals that make technicians search through prose before locating a component.
- Marketing dashboards, oversized cards, decorative gradients, glass effects, and AI-first chat surfaces.
- Large translucent rectangles that obscure the point map or imply imprecise module boundaries.
- Decorative pseudo-3D that distorts coordinates or makes measurement points harder to locate.
- Approval and calibration steps exposed to technicians when the supplied in-house material is already the accepted source.

## Design Principles

1. The board is the interface. Keep it large, inspectable, and central to every diagnosis step.
2. Preserve source truth. Geometry, labels, and test locations come from the supplied point map, schematic, and repair guide.
3. Reveal detail progressively. Show the fault path first, then the module, test point, and measurement instruction at the moment they are needed.
4. Use depth to explain hierarchy. The 2.5D treatment separates board, module, fault path, and inspection layers without changing coordinates.
5. Optimize for bench use. Interactions must be quick, readable in a bright workshop, and usable on desktop and narrow screens.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA for technician-facing controls and text. Do not use color as the only fault or selection signal; pair it with outlines, labels, and numbered markers. Keep focus states visible, support keyboard operation, respect reduced-motion preferences, use touch targets of at least 40 px where space permits, and keep terminology ready for later localization.
